# Equipment models — reference check and application integration

Reference geometry checked 27 October 2026. The sandbox and the application's 3D/Split views share recognizable examples of chemical-process equipment. These are authored visual models, not dimensioned reproductions or validated mechanical designs. Feed and Product are stream-boundary concepts in the flowsheet; supply and receiver vessels are the selected physical representations.

## Construction and motion corrections

- Gauges have raised mounting stems and unobstructed full dial faces. Outlet hardware clears adjacent vessel heads.
- Flanges and sanitary fittings have open bores; bolts sit on the annular metal rather than inside the bore.
- The mixer rotates about the horizontal spindle. Its vane sweep clears the bowl and the stationary pickup. The axisymmetric bowl uses the fixed cut plane in the inspection view.
- The default transparent inspection mode reveals the complete shell at 16% opacity while keeping supports, flanges, motors, gauges and internals solid. Section view still cuts the metal walls; switching both inspection modes off restores opaque exteriors. This transparency is a viewing aid, not a claim that the real equipment is made from glass. Fluid remains depth-tested against the surrounding hardware.
- The reactor packing remains fixed. Fans and mixer internals rotate; valve handwheels remain still during steady operation.
- Column liquid spills through downcomers, small bubbles form within tray liquid, and the thermal equipment shows films/boiling inside its casing. Earlier floating colored tracer dots were removed.
- Playback pause freezes the same operation clock used by rotors and fluid shaders. Motion also stops when the browser tab or 3D viewport is hidden, then resumes without a time jump. Mixer rotation is deliberately slowed for inspection; the speed buttons are playback multipliers, not motor operating setpoints.

The supplied laboratory video was inspected for glass, clamp, tubing and frame construction. Its precise apparatus identity and flow conditions were not inferred from appearance. Industrial construction remains the selected equipment family. The user subsequently requested greater transparency and more vigorous animation, so transparent inspection is now the default and the operation uses faster circulation, stronger liquid surfaces and denser bubbling.

## Scope of accuracy

Construction types and flow topology are reference-informed. Absolute dimensions, stress, clearances under load, hydraulics, thermal performance, interfacial transport and motor speed are not engineering-certified. The fluid animation is a visual operating illustration, not CFD. Manufacturer photos and the private user video are reference material; they are not redistributed as app assets.

## Transparent flow and connected examples

The latest revision uses a common route definition for each pipe wall and animated liquid core, including rounded bends. Clear walls remain at 16% opacity; flanges, supports and valve operators remain solid. Liquid uses stronger teal, amber and blue pigments with continuous advecting highlights. Colors identify illustrative streams and heat-transfer transitions, not calculated temperatures or concentrations. Playback now offers 0.5×, 1×, 2× and 4×. The five smaller connected examples are Simple heating, Mixing & cooling, Heated reactor train, Distillation train and Flow distribution; repeated feeds/receivers have unique instance IDs and tags.

## App-facing tray-count parameter

EquipmentModel accepts trayCount as a numeric prop. For example, within an existing React Three Fiber Canvas:

<EquipmentModel kind="Distillation" trayCount={20} section={false}
  transparent playing speed={1} motionVisible />

The pure geometry API is createEquipmentModel("Distillation", false, true, { trayCount: 20 }). When using the pure API, pass the same options as the fifth argument of createProcessContents. columnLayout is the single source for tray elevations, shell height, access platforms, feed elevation and overhead assembly elevation. getSystem("distillation", 20) also re-routes the connected feed and distillate lines.

The sandbox's Number of trays control accepts integers from 2 to 80, default 8. The public layout helper normalizes out-of-range/fractional inputs and falls back to 8 for non-finite values. Tray spacing is preserved; hardware is not stretched. Adding trays rebuilds the physical tray stack, downcomers, liquid surfaces and bubbles, extends the shell/ladder, adds service platforms, raises the condenser and gauge, relocates the feed, and refits the camera. Bubble density is bounded for taller columns. The component preserves its operation clock when the count changes.

The application now stores this visual parameter as unit.params.trayCount. The schema accepts integers from 2 to 80 and defaults missing values to 8, including older projects. Select a distillation column, edit Visible trays (3D) in its Setup inspector, and choose Apply conditions. Each column retains its own count through validated actions, local saving, JSON export and reload. The count sizes the physical model and fitting heights; the solver still calculates one ideal equilibrium contact and does not predict a multistage column.

## Large examples and compact plant arrangements — 28 October 2026

The sandbox now opens on Integrated process plant (25 assemblies, 25 streams). Parallel reaction & finishing (17 assemblies, 17 streams) and Three-column fractionation (21 assemblies, 20 streams) join the five smaller examples. Every process retains unique equipment tags and fully connected, separately addressable inlet/outlet ports. Transparent shells and connecting pipes are on initially and when a different example is chosen. An Inspect equipment selector makes every assembly reachable even when projected labels would overlap. The tray count applies to every column in the current example; getSystem("integrated", 20) and the other large-system IDs regenerate elevated column connections as well.

The large examples use compact authored plot plans instead of a 5.5-unit traversal grid. Related equipment occupies shared process bays: tall reactors and separation columns along the rear, perpendicular rotating/thermal equipment facing a central pipe corridor, and receivers along the outer edge. Quarter-turn orientation is shared by the rendered models, routing envelopes and actual nozzle coordinates. Concrete plinths follow individual assembly footprints. An open central aisle runs below a three-tier steel pipe rack; every longer connection receives its own rack lane, with local connections routed directly. Hardware and supports remain opaque while pipe walls and process enclosures remain transparent.

Plant-layout references consulted for this revision:
- HSE, Plant Layout: https://www.hse.gov.uk/comah/sragtech/techmeasplantlay.htm — balance shorter material transfer with operability, maintenance and access.
- AXA XL Risk Consulting, Oil and Chemical Plant Layout and Spacing, PRC.2.5.2: https://axaxl.com/-/media/axaxl/files/pdfs/prc-guidelines/prc-2/prc252oilandchemicalplantlayoutandspacingv1.pdf — a central pipe rack with vessels/reactors outward and access around equipment.

These sources inform the arrangement concept, not the sandbox's distances. The model does not implement hazard-dependent separation, tube-bundle withdrawal space, fire protection or pressure/transfer design. The layouts are compact illustrative arrangements, not a claim of a global mathematical minimum or a construction-ready plant. The UI's footprint comparison measures the axis-aligned XZ bounding rectangle of the authored equipment routing envelopes, against the previous row arrangement using those same envelopes; it excludes piping, roads and site setbacks from both measurements.

Routing uses an orthogonal search around equipment, rack steel, reserved nozzle leads, allocated trunk lanes and existing pipes. Independent lines crossing in projection remain at separate elevations; the renderer and continuous fluid shader consume the same rounded route. Regression checks cover footprint reduction, non-overlapping assembly envelopes, rack-steel clearance, unique ports, complete graph reachability, aligned rotated nozzles, inter-pipe clearance and column resizing from 2 to 80 trays.

## Application integration — 28 October 2026

In the main workspace at /, choose 3D to explore the current project or Split to keep its editable 2D flowsheet alongside the detailed equipment. Both use the same eight models and continuous fluid rendering as /sandbox/equipment. Transparent shell starts on whenever the 3D view mounts; the display controls provide Fit plant, the transparency toggle, Play flow/Pause flow, and 0.5×, 1×, 2× or 4× playback. Motion starts paused. Selecting a model or its label opens that project's unit inspector; projects with more than ten units also offer Inspect equipment.

Templates now includes Integrated process plant (25 units / 25 streams), Parallel reaction & finishing (17 / 17), and Three-column fractionation (21 / 20) as editable application projects. Their lightweight definitions are shared with the sandbox. Opening Templates does not load the WebGL renderer; the workspace loads it lazily for 3D or Split.

The current project's unit IDs, names, edge topology, physical port mapping, stream colors and individual column tray counts supply the 3D scene. Projects whose IDs and equipment kinds match a large template reuse its curated placements while retaining their current connections and parameters. Other user graphs receive a generated compact arrangement; disconnected equipment remains visible while editing. This arrangement is derived for viewing and never rewrites saved 2D positions. Its cache ignores 2D position changes and operating conditions that do not affect visible geometry, so those edits do not reroute pipes or reset the camera.

The renderer retains the editor's Figtree controls, green light theme and existing black dark mode. If the graphics context is lost, Reload preview recreates the local 3D scene without reloading the application or replacing the project. A project that cannot be arranged in 3D remains editable in 2D. Geometry, transparent flow and playback continue to be illustrations; the production solver and its documented ideal-model limits are unchanged.
