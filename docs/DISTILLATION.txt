DISTILLATION — FUG AND MESH

Open a distillation tower's Properties → Setup → Column calculation method.

Agents and HTTP clients use the same schemas and solver. See AI_GUIDE.txt,
GET /api/ai/guide and GET /api/capabilities for the complete contract. Agent
update actions merge sparse distillation/hydraulics settings; a diameter-only
edit preserves method, operating point, fluid properties and other geometry.
POST /api/actions/preview returns a validated candidate project without saving;
POST /api/simulate accepts that complete project and returns all tray results at
equipment[columnId].distillation.hydraulics. In-app proposals still require
Apply reviewed actions before saving, followed by a workspace run to refresh
the 3D result. No hydraulic feedback into the separation solver is enabled.
Choose Shortcut design (FUG) or Equilibrium stages (MESH), apply conditions,
and run the simulation. The tower's Results tab reports the selected solver.
Templates → Distillation · FUG shortcut / Distillation · MESH column provide
complete benzene–toluene examples at 55 kPa without a database lookup.

An omitted params.distillation retains the original single-contact/TP-flash
behavior, so existing files and templates keep their previous calculations.
Both new modes persist through project files, actions, revisions and reload.
Switching between FUG and MESH preserves the inactive method's specifications.

FUG
Supply distinct adjacent light/heavy keys, relative volatilities for all active
feed species, light-key recovery to distillate, heavy-key recovery to bottoms,
feed liquid fraction q, column pressure and specified product temperatures.
Reflux can be an operating L/D or a multiple (>1) of calculated minimum reflux.
Recoveries refer to fractions of each feed component, not product purities.

The TypeScript shortcut solver uses the following calculation sequence:
- Normalize alpha to the heavy key; Fenske gives minimum equilibrium stages.
- Hengstebeck–Geddes gives nonkey recoveries with stable log-odds evaluation.
- Solve sum(alpha_i*z_i/(alpha_i-theta))=1-q between adjacent keys.
- Rmin=sum(alpha_i*xD_i/(alpha_i-theta))-1; require positive Rmin and R>Rmin.
- X=(R-Rmin)/(R+1); Y=1-exp((1+54.4X)/(11+117.2X)*(X-1)/sqrt(X)).
  The stage estimate is ceil((Nmin+Y)/(1-Y)).
Reported stages include the equilibrium reboiler and exclude the total condenser.
Installed tray count controls appearance only in FUG. Feed q is an explicit
assumption, not computed from feed temperature. No azeotrope prediction,
stage profile, hydraulic rating, feed-tray design or separate end duties.
Net equipment duty is only outlet-minus-feed enthalpy at the supplied product
temperatures under the project's selected property model; it is labeled as such.

MESH
Specify 2–80 ideal equilibrium trays, feed tray counted from the top (1..N),
top pressure, optional total pressure drop, operating reflux L/D, and distillate
molar flow. Distillate must leave positive bottoms. Tray count drives both views
and the solved stages; condenser and reboiler are additional end units.
Feed pressure must reach the feed-tray pressure. Pressure varies linearly from
condenser to reboiler. Only one feed and two liquid products are exposed.

The Rust implementation solves simultaneous stage equations with sparse
factorization. Its property adapter uses the shared Raoult or Modified
UNIFAC 2.0 vapor-pressure, gas-Cp, latent-heat and excess-enthalpy calculations.
The gas-only model cannot run MESH. All active component data and every stage
must remain supported by the selected model (1–200 kPa for liquid models).
The app's catalog limit remains 40 selected components. Absent components are
removed from the numerical system and restored as exact zero product flows.

Positive log-flow/composition variables and bounded temperatures feed a sparse
Newton solve with backtracking and bounded damped least-squares fallback. The
adapter uses colored finite differences for phase-property derivatives and
exact flow derivatives. Material residuals are scaled by each species' feed,
so trace species are not hidden by bulk flow. Energy scaling is numerical:
40000 J/mol * feed rate; this is not a thermodynamic property. All stage and
overall balances must pass; tolerances are capped at 1e-7. Default is 1e-8,
with a tighter internal iteration target. The 200-evaluation default budget
counts nonlinear trial evaluations, excluding Jacobian/final verification.

Every accepted stage is independently checked with the existing TP flash of
an equal-mole liquid/vapor blend. Phase compositions and enthalpies must agree.
This checks VLE consistency without a tangent-plane stability search.
There is no LLE/VLLE stability test, efficiency, hydraulic feedback, side draw,
multiple-feed support, partial condenser, reaction equilibrium or mechanical
design. Convergence is not experimental/industrial validation. Property and
parameter warnings are retained, including estimated latent heat and UNIFAC
parameter licensing. Missing data, dry stages, wrong duty signs, unsupported
pressure, insufficient feed pressure or nonconvergence return errors.

Results include component identities, feed data/enthalpy, stage T/P, liquid and
vapor flows/compositions/enthalpies, stage and overall residuals, condenser and
reboiler duties and solver identity. Positive duty adds heat. The condenser's
liquid flow is reflux and its vapor composition is incipient equilibrium vapor;
there is no condenser vapor outlet. Reboiler liquid flow is bottoms. Equipment
duty is the sum of condenser and reboiler duties. Shared property provenance
includes the rebuilt WebAssembly hash and the selected data/model identity.

OPTIONAL SIEVE-TRAY HYDRAULIC RATING
In MESH Setup enable Predict tray flooding and weeping. Set diameter and tray
spacing, supply liquid density (kg/m3) and surface tension (N/m) at representative
tray conditions, and edit the sieve preset under geometry. Apply and run.
Results lists per-tray jet flooding, downcomer backup and weep onset, including
unrated checks. 80% of either flooding limit is a warning; >=100% predicts flood.
Actual hole velocity / predicted weep velocity <1 predicts weeping. Both flags
can occur together. Convergence alone does not imply acceptable hydraulics.

This is a POST-SOLVE rating of local exiting MESH tray traffic. No equilibrium
stage, stream, efficiency, pressure profile, purity or duty is changed. The
calculated hydraulic tray pressure drop is reported separately for diagnosis.
FUG and single-contact modes have no per-tray rating. Disabled and omitted
settings preserve previous simulation outputs. Missing properties produce an
unavailable rating; out-of-correlation conditions produce partial results,
never a false all-clear. This preliminary model needs comparison against
vendor/experimental data before use for equipment design.

Geometry is one-pass, chordal downcomer sieve trays. The downcomer segment has
angle theta satisfying theta-sin(theta)=2*pi*f_dc; weir length=D*sin(theta/2).
Net vapor area is A_t-A_dc; active area excludes both inlet seal and outlet
area, A_t-2*A_dc; hole area is holeAreaFraction*activeArea. Defaults: diameter
1.2 m, spacing 0.46 m, 12% downcomer, 10% hole area, 5 mm holes, 50 mm weir,
40 mm under-apron clearance and discharge coefficient 0.75. These are editable
geometry assumptions, not supplied material properties. Supported geometry:
300-900 mm spacing, 3-12.7 mm holes and 10-15% open area. Clearance must not
exceed weir height. Other tray types, multiple passes and fouling are unsupported.

Liquid properties are explicit constants across trays; component molecular
weights and each tray's T, P and composition give ideal-gas vapor density.
Fair capacity uses Simevo's published natural-log polynomial fit and the
(sigma/0.02)^0.2 correction, with sigma in N/m. Our implemented domain is
0.01<=FLV<=2 and 300<=spacing<=900 mm; no extrapolation. Capacity is based on
net area; open area restriction avoids applying the fit below 10% hole area.
Francis weir crest =0.75*(QL/weirLength)^(2/3) m, QL in m3/s.
Dry head =0.051*(holeVelocity/Co)^2*rhoV/rhoL m.
Residual head =12.5/rhoL m; total tray head=dry+weir+crest+residual.
Downcomer loss =0.166*(QL/min(A_dc,clearance*weirLength))^2 m.
Backup =2*(weir+crest+totalTrayHead+downcomerLoss), using 50% liquid fraction.
Backup flood fraction=backup/(spacing+weirHeight). Pressure=totalHead*rhoL*g.
Eduljee weep velocity=[K2-0.9*(25.4-holeDiameter_mm)]/sqrt(rhoV).
K2 uses approximate readings of Figure 8 from Minhas (2025):
clear liquid mm: 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 80, 100, 110
K2:             27.3,28.35,29.12,29.65,30.02,30.32,30.75,31,31.12
Interpolation is linear and limited to 15-110 mm. No computed weep rate,
entrainment rate, downcomer choke, dynamic holdup or separation penalty.

In the project 3D view, configured diameter scales the schematic assembly
radially; spacing changes tray elevations and shell height. Connected ports
and routing use the same geometry. Active sieve geometry omits valve caps.
Tray 1 is at the top. Rings show hydraulic status; droplets show weeping,
upward carryover and deeper illustrated liquid show flooding. Visual levels
beyond the limit are bounded illustrative effects, not transient predictions.
Only a successful current simulation supplies hydraulic effects; incomplete
ratings are identified and unavailable checks do not animate a guessed fault.

diameter/spacing/open-area trends; invalid inputs; unsupported ranges;
serialization; and exact equality of pre/post-rating separation outputs.
3D tests check tray numbering, fault effects, dimensional ports and cache
invalidation. Browser coverage exercises setup, all three operating regimes,
3D display, reload persistence and disabling. These are implementation checks,
not experimental validation of the correlations for a particular mixture.
